Eureka · quick capture for macOS · Obsidian and Apple Notes

Press a hotkey. Type a thought. Done.

Eureka is a free, open-source macOS menu bar app for quick capture: press ⌥T in any app to save a one-line thought, with the text you selected and its source (page URL or app), to Obsidian or Apple Notes. ⌥R does the same with a screenshot.

Download for Mac Source on GitHub

Version 2.1.0 · macOS 12 or later · Apple Silicon and Intel · MIT license

Ideas show up while you are reading, coding or in a meeting — not while your notes app is open. Switching apps to write one line costs more focus than the line is worth, so most of those thoughts are lost. Eureka makes the cost one hotkey and one line, and it remembers where the thought came from, so the note still makes sense next week.

Three ways to capture

⌥ T  A thought, with its context

Press the hotkey in any app and a small input appears at your cursor. Whatever you had selected is quoted under your thought, with the page URL (Safari, Chrome, Edge, Brave, Arc) or the app name as the source. Select nothing and it is just a quick note. Enter saves, Esc cancels — you never leave what you were doing.

⌥ R  A screenshot, with one line

Drag a region, say why it matters, press Enter. The image and your comment land in the same card, so the chart you grabbed in a meeting still has its “this spike = the 9am push” next to it a month later. Enter on an empty line saves just the image.

/  Ask AI about what you selected

Start the line with / and it becomes a question instead of a note. The selected text is sent along as context and a short answer appears in the panel. Bring your own key: DeepSeek, OpenAI, or a local model through Ollama.

Or skip the hotkey: click the dot

Select text with the mouse and a small blue dot appears next to the pointer. Click it and the same panel opens with your selection attached. Ignore it and it fades away. It can be turned off in Settings.

Also in the box

  • Recent captures — a floating bubble keeps your last 20 thoughts; click one to jump to it.
  • Obsidian or Apple Notes — plain Markdown in your vault, or a daily note in Notes.
  • Yours to rebind — both hotkeys are configurable, and every setting is a defaults key.

What lands in your notes

One file per day, one Obsidian callout per thought, plain Markdown that stays readable in any editor:

your-vault/Eureka/
  2026-06-29/
    Thoughts.md       # every thought of the day
    attachments/      # screenshots

The colored cards come from a small CSS snippet that Eureka installs into .obsidian/snippets/ when you pick your vault. With Apple Notes, thoughts are appended to a note called Thoughts — YYYY-MM-DD.

Install

Requires macOS 12 or later (Apple Silicon and Intel). Paste this into Terminal:

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Claire1217/Eureka/main/install.sh | bash

Or download the .zip from Releases, unzip it into /Applications and run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Eureka.app (the app is not notarized yet).

On first launch, enable Eureka under System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility so it can read the text you select, then pick your Obsidian vault or Apple Notes. Press ⌥ T and you are done; everything else is under the E! menu bar icon.

Privacy

Everything is written locally — to your vault folder or to Apple Notes. Eureka has no server, no account and no analytics. Network requests go only to the AI endpoint you configure, when you ask a / question. Whatever was already on your clipboard is never saved: when an app does not expose its selection, Eureka sends a ⌘C to read it and puts your previous clipboard back right after.

FAQ

Is Eureka free and open source?

Yes. Eureka is MIT-licensed, with no account, no paid tier and no server of its own. The only possible cost is the AI provider you choose for / questions, billed by that provider; a local model through Ollama costs nothing, and capturing thoughts never needs AI at all.

Does it work with Obsidian? Does Obsidian need to be open?

Yes, Obsidian is the default. Eureka writes plain Markdown straight into your vault: one file per day, by default Eureka/YYYY-MM-DD/Thoughts.md, with screenshots in attachments/ next to it. It writes the file itself, so Obsidian does not need to be running. Each thought is an Obsidian callout, and a small CSS snippet turns it into a colored card. Clicking a thought in the recent-captures bubble opens it in Obsidian.

Can I save to Apple Notes instead?

Yes. Choose Apple Notes on first launch or in Settings. Thoughts are appended to one note per day, Thoughts — YYYY-MM-DD, and macOS asks for permission to let Eureka control Notes. Screenshots are kept in ~/Pictures/Eureka/ and the note gets the file path, because Notes drops earlier images when a note is changed through automation.

What about Logseq, or another Markdown app?

In Settings you can point Eureka at any folder, not only an Obsidian vault, and it writes the same plain Markdown files there, so any app that reads a folder of .md files can open them. Two things are Obsidian-only: the colored cards (elsewhere a thought shows as a quote whose first line is [!thought-coral] 11:03) and jumping to a thought from the recent-captures bubble. There is no integration with Notion, Bear or other apps that keep notes in a database.

Which apps does it capture the selection and source URL from?

The selected text comes from any app that exposes it through macOS Accessibility; when an app does not (some Electron apps), Eureka sends a ⌘C and restores your clipboard right after. The page URL is read from Safari, Chrome, Edge, Brave and Arc. In every other app, including Firefox, the source is the app name.

Does it send my notes anywhere?

No. Thoughts and screenshots are written only to your vault folder or to Apple Notes. Eureka has no analytics and makes no network requests of its own. The only requests go to the AI endpoint you configure, and only when you use it: a / question sends the question and the selected text.

Can I use a local model, like Ollama?

Yes. Set the API base URL to http://localhost:11434/v1, pick a model such as llama3.2, and leave the API key empty; nothing leaves your Mac. Any OpenAI-compatible API works the same way, including DeepSeek and OpenAI (setup).

Which Macs does it run on?

macOS 12 Monterey or later, on Apple Silicon and Intel: the release is a universal binary. It needs the Accessibility permission to read your selection. Releases are not notarized yet, so remove the quarantine flag after downloading (the install script does this for you).

How is it different from Apple Quick Note, Raycast or Drafts?

Those open a notes window, and the note lives in their app. Eureka opens a one-line input at your cursor, attaches the text you selected and where it came from without being asked, and writes into your Obsidian vault or Apple Notes, then gets out of the way. It does one thing; if you want a full writing app or a launcher, those tools do much more.

Is this the same as Netflix Eureka, or the other projects named Eureka?

No. This Eureka (repository Claire1217/Eureka) is a macOS menu bar app for capturing thoughts into Obsidian or Apple Notes. It is unrelated to Netflix Eureka, the service registry used with Spring Cloud, to the Eureka form library for iOS, to the Eureka Math curriculum, and to other apps and repositories with the same name. To find this one, search for Claire1217/Eureka or Eureka macOS Obsidian.
